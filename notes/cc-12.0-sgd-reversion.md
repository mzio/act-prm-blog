# cc-12.0 — the SGD reversion: can ANY Stage-2 SFT avoid degrading agentic behaviour?

Started 2026-09-01. **Status: training COMPLETE (1e-3, 3e-3); rollouts IN FLIGHT.**

Companion to [cc-11.0](cc-11.0-lr1e4-multidomain-plan.md), whose multi-domain lr 1e-4
sweep is **paused** by request so this question can be answered first.

---

## The question

Every Stage-2 checkpoint we have measured with AdamW rolls out at or below the base
model. Base = 14.3% retail / 55.6% airline. The only Stage-2 checkpoints that ever beat
base (20.5% / 72.2%) were SGD-era ones — but we then established *why* they looked good:

> they were **badly trained**. SGD 3e-3 on the old `sft` path reached eval action-PPL
> 2.3418 against a base of 3.8428, took until b149 to get there, and had only banked 4%
> of its gain by b10 (AdamW banks 81% by b5). Its train PPL got *worse* over the run
> (2.192 → 2.503).

That leaves two incompatible readings, and they have different consequences:

- **(A) Under-fitting is the active ingredient.** The good rollouts came from barely
  moving the weights. Then *any* well-fit Stage-2 SFT degrades the policy, and the
  Stage-2 objective is simply misaligned with agentic competence.
- **(B) SGD is the active ingredient.** Something about the *shape* of the SGD update
  (sparse: max/median |B@A| ratio 33–91×) preserves behaviour that AdamW's uniform
  update (ratio 1.4–2.1×) destroys, independent of how far it fits.

The old SGD results cannot separate these, because they confound optimizer, LR, trainer
path (`sft`, not `sft_flat`), corpus generation, batching, and shuffling all at once.

## Design — one variable

Take the **current** `sft_flat` configuration, which we trust, and change **only the
optimizer** (and its LR). Everything else is preserved exactly as the in-flight AdamW
runs had it:

| held fixed | value |
|---|---|
| trainer | `sft_flat` (corpus-wide flat step sampling) |
| shuffling | corpus-wide **and** trajectory-wide, seed advanced per epoch |
| `steps_per_batch` | 32 |
| `num_batches` | 200 |
| `eval_every` / `save_every` | 5 / 10 |
| observations | hidden (`--hide_observations`) |
| LoRA | r8 / a16 / linear |
| corpus | the new AdamW-trained Stage-1 traces |
| arms | `actions_only`, `thoughts_policy_adamw30` |
| selection metric | `eval_actiononly_ppl` (action sub-span only) |

**Changed:** `--optimizer sgd`, `--learning_rate {1e-3, 3e-3}`.

This is a genuine one-variable contrast against the AdamW lr 1e-3 / 1e-4 runs on the same
corpus, so it discriminates (A) from (B) in a way the historical comparison could not.

### Why these two LRs

**1e-3** brackets from below: it sits between the SGD LR behind the good checkpoints
(3e-3) and the AdamW LR (1e-3), so if the *optimizer shape* matters, 1e-3 SGD should
already show it.

**3e-3** is the historical value. Its provenance is worth stating plainly, because it is
weaker than it looks: it was chosen by a 30-batch probe on retail `thoughts_base` over
`{1e-4, 1e-3, 3e-3}` (eval gain +1.19% → +6.94%), where it was **the top of the probed
range**. It is a boundary value, not a located optimum — nothing above it was ever tried.
That probe also predates the finding that `--optimizer` was silently defaulting to `sgd`,
so "3e-3" was always an SGD number; under AdamW (update ≈ `lr·sign(grad)`) it would be an
enormous step. **Open:** 5e-3 / 1e-2 remain unprobed. Worth doing only if 3e-3 rolls out
well, since the point of the arc is to reach the *known-good* regime, not to optimise PPL.

## Training results (both LRs, both arms, retail)

All four runs were **cap-limited at b199 and never plateaued** — early stopping never
fired. References: base **3.8428**, old SGD 3e-3 (`sft` path) **2.3418**, AdamW 1e-4
**1.7538**, AdamW 1e-3 **1.7670**.

| run | eval action-PPL (b5 → best) | eval acc | train action-PPL |
|---|---|---|---|
| SGD 1e-3 `actions_only` | → **2.7517** @b199 | .7681 | — |
| SGD 1e-3 `thoughts_policy` | → **2.4625** @b199 | — | — |
| SGD 3e-3 `actions_only` | 3.8258 → **2.2983** @b199 | .7608 → .7751 | 3.72 → 1.95 |
| SGD 3e-3 `thoughts_policy` | 3.0982 → **2.2822** @b199 | .7731 → .7809 | 2.42 → 1.79 |

Train PPL is quoted as **windowed means (b0–24 → b175–199)**, not first/last batch — see
the retraction below. Both arms fall monotonically and flatten around b100.

**SGD 3e-3 lands on the old eval PPL** (2.298 / 2.282 vs 2.3418) — far short of AdamW's
1.75. The reversion recreated the under-fit, not merely the LR. Note `thoughts_policy`
reaches a *better* fit than `actions_only` on both train (1.79 vs 1.95) and eval action
span (2.2822 vs 2.2983), despite spending part of its gradient on thought tokens.

Gain is also back-loaded the way the old runs were: `actions_only` @3e-3 had only 49% of
its total gain by b50 (`thoughts_policy` 62%), against AdamW's 81% by **b5**.

### Weight movement — the reversion hit the target regime

`median |B@A|` is the discriminator (max alone does not separate the runs). SGD 3e-3
`actions_only`:

| snapshot | eval PPL | max\|B@A\| | median\|B@A\| |
|---|---|---|---|
| step_0010 | 3.7917 | 2.301e-04 | 8.045e-06 |
| step_0050 | 3.0807 | 1.319e-03 | 3.297e-05 |
| step_0100 | 2.4652 | 3.252e-03 | 4.728e-05 |
| step_0200 | 2.2983 | 4.395e-03 | **5.420e-05** |

Reference points: old SGD 3e-3 `step_best` (converses, 21.4%) = **4.7e-05**; AdamW 1e-3
`step_0020` (0% prose) = 1.46e-03; AdamW 1e-3 `step_best` = 2.19e-03.

So step_0200 sits **within 15% of the known-good checkpoint's median movement** and ~27×
below the collapsed AdamW one, with a max/median ratio of **81×** — the sparse-update
signature (AdamW: 1.4–2.1×). SGD 1e-3 `actions_only` step_0200 is 3.897e-05, ratio ~50×.

**This is the key setup fact:** the new SGD checkpoints are a close weight-space match for
the historically good ones, on the current corpus and pipeline. If they roll out well,
the answer is (A)/(B) and Stage-2 is recoverable in the under-fit regime; if they roll out
at base or below, the old 20.5% was a property of the *old corpus or old trainer path*,
and neither optimizer nor under-fitting is the explanation.

## Rollouts (in flight)

4 rollouts at SGD 1e-3: {`actions_only`, `thoughts_policy_adamw30`} × {`step_0020`,
`step_best`}, retail hold-out, 42 tasks, `regime=hide`, `turns=20`. Trajectories,
completions and scores are written under distinct run tags
(`retail_rollout_<arm>_sgdlr1e_3_<step>`) so **no prior rollout artifacts are
overwritten** — the `.done` marker collision that previously let SGD-era and `sft_flat`
rollouts share a tag is avoided by `CKPT_TAG`/`CKPT_STEP`.

Primary read is **user-turns/episode**, not the completion count: it is monotone with
completion across every checkpoint measured so far (4.53 → 3.61 → 2.05 tracking 21.4% →
11.9% → 0.0%) and is far better powered than a 42-task binary at Wilson CIs of ±10pts.

### Result 1 — SGD 1e-3 `thoughts_policy` / `step_0020` (42/42 tasks, complete)

| checkpoint | solved | rate | Wilson 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| base (no Stage-2) | 6/42 | 14.3% | [6.7%, 27.8%] |
| AdamW 1e-3 `step_0020` | 5/42 | 11.9% | [5.2%, 25.0%] |
| AdamW 1e-3 `step_best` (PPL-optimal) | 0/42 | 0.0% | [0.0%, 8.4%] |
| **SGD 1e-3 `step_0020`** | **13/42** | **31.0%** | **[19.1%, 46.0%]** |

Fisher two-sided vs SGD 1e-3 `step_0020`: AdamW `step_best` **p=0.0001**, AdamW
`step_0020` p=0.0608, base **p=0.1163**.

**What this does and does not support.** It is the highest Stage-2 completion we have
measured and it decisively escapes the AdamW `step_best` collapse. It does **not**
establish an improvement over base — p=0.116, and the CIs overlap substantially. The
honest reading is: *the under-fit SGD regime avoids the degradation; whether it exceeds
base needs more tasks.* Raising n (the 126-task variant) is the cheapest way to settle it.

### Result 2 — the rest of the SGD 1e-3 ladder

| arm / snapshot | completion | user-turns/ep |
|---|---|---|
| `thoughts_policy` / `step_0020` | **31.0%** (13/42) | *(no trajectories — predates the dump fix)* |
| `thoughts_policy` / `step_best` | 23.8% (10/42) | 6.21 |
| `actions_only` / `step_0020` | 21.4% (9/42) | 6.14 |
| `actions_only` / `step_best` | *(running)* | |

Every SGD 1e-3 checkpoint measured sits **above base (14.3%)**, and far above the AdamW
equivalents (11.9% / 0.0%). Note the `actions_only` baseline also recovers — so this is
not a property of the thought corpus, it is a property of the optimizer/fit regime.

### RETRACTION — user-turns/ep is not the mechanism

The between-checkpoint ladder is real and monotone (6.21 → 4.53 → 3.61 → 2.05 against
23.8% → 21.4% → 11.9% → 0.0%). But the **within-checkpoint** data contradicts a causal
reading, in both arms that have trajectories:

| checkpoint | user/ep all | solved | failed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `thoughts_policy` / `step_best` | 6.21 | 6.20 (n=10) | 6.22 (n=32) |
| `actions_only` / `step_0020` | 6.14 | **5.22** (n=9) | **6.39** (n=33) |

Flat in one arm, **reversed** in the other — solved episodes use *fewer* user turns. The
mundane explanation is that failed episodes drag on unresolved and accumulate turns, so
within a run the statistic tracks episode length, not competence.

Corrected claim: user-turns/ep **separates collapsed policies from functional ones** (a
policy at 2.05 has genuinely stopped conversing) but **does not predict success among
functional policies**. "The policy stops asking the user, so it cannot finish" is a valid
description of the AdamW collapse; it is NOT a general mechanism, and it should not be
used to rank healthy checkpoints. Completion remains the ground truth.

This is nonetheless evidence for reading **(A)** over **(B)**-as-magic: the collapse
tracks how far Stage-2 fits, and an under-fit checkpoint on the *current* corpus and
*current* `sft_flat` pipeline recovers the historical behaviour. The old 20.5% was not an
artifact of the old corpus or old trainer path.

| 1 | **Checkpoint glob never matched.** All 4 SGD 1e-3 rollouts logged "no checkpoint, skip", the driver exited 0, and the 3e-3 chain fired early on an empty result. | — | The glob in `run_sgd_flat.sh` was `${lrtag}_sgd_nb200_flat32sgd`; the dirs are `${lrtag}_nb200_flat32sgd`. Corrected and verified FOUND for both arms before relaunch. |
| 2 | **Chain script deadlocked.** `chain_sgd1e3_rollouts.sh` waited on `ps -eo args \| grep -qE '[r]un_sgd_flat\.sh\|[c]hain_sgd3e3\.sh'`. It sat at its first log line for 3.5h while the GPU was idle 23 min. | assume it died; restart | It had **not** died. The `[r]un…` bracket trick defeats self-matching for *grep's own* argv, but my Claude Code bash wrapper's argv contained the entire heredoc that created the script — including the literal pattern — so the match was permanent. Fix: abandon the chain, launch `run_sgd_flat.sh` directly, then `kill -9` the chain so it could not later `rm` the markers and re-run all four rollouts. |
| 3 | **`pgrep -f <name>` self-matching** (recurring, ~4 separate false "alive" readings). | — | `ps -eo pid,args \| awk '$2=="bash" && $3 ~ /script\.sh$/'`. Note even this is not foolproof — a bare `\|\| echo` fallback after it printed a misleading "confirmed dead" line while the process was in fact alive. Read the awk output, do not trust a fallback echo. |
| 4 | **Orphaned CUDA children.** `kill -9` on a driver leaves `main_pytorch.py` holding the GPU (3+ occurrences, once blocking a sweep 13 min). | — | Kill the process *group*, or the explicit child PIDs. `run_sgd_flat.sh` now sweeps `pgrep -f 'main_pytorch\.py'` before each rollout. |
| 5 | **RETRACTED: "SGD makes train PPL worse."** I reported `thoughts_policy` @3e-3 going 1.6399 → 1.8271 and called it a reproduction of the old SGD run's 2.192 → 2.503. Both are artifacts. | — | `train/*_ppl` is a **per-batch** statistic. `sft_flat` draws a fresh random 32-step batch each time (sd 0.45 in b0–24, range 1.64–3.28), and the old `sft` path batched per-trajectory at bs=4 (sd **1.2–3.8**, maxima to 22.4). 1.6399 was the *minimum* of the first window, 1.8271 one late draw against a mean of 1.793. **Always window this metric** (25-batch means) before reading a trend; first-vs-last is meaningless here. Windowed, every run improves monotonically. |
| 6 | **`rollouts_per_task.jsonl` mixes splits.** The file had **44 rows for 42 eval tasks**, so a naive `mean(final_reward)` gave 29.5–31.7% depending on when it was read. | — | 42 eval rows + **2 train rows** (1 train task × 2 generations): `rl.py` deliberately generates train rollouts even in rollout-only mode, and the env carries `train: 1 task`. **Always filter `split=="eval"`** before scoring; also dedupe on `task_id` since `gen_id` can exceed 0. Correct figure 13/42 = 31.0%. |
| 7 | **Trajectories were never saved.** The user asked for trajectories + completions + scores; only scores existed. `save_rollouts_every: 0`, and `logs.log` holds reset observations + LiteLLM noise, not conversations. | use the built-in `generate_and_save_trajectories` | Rejected — it runs a **second generation pass** (~80 min/rollout) and re-generates rather than capturing the episodes actually scored. ## Caveats on the conclusion this arc can support

- **n=42 binary is noisy.** The same checkpoint scored 11.9% at n=42 and 15.1% at n=126.
  Differences of 1–3 tasks are not interpretable; lean on user-turns/episode.
- **Retail only.** Whether this transfers is what the paused cc-11.0 sweep would answer.
- **3e-3 is a boundary value** (see above), so "SGD 3e-3 is best" is not a claim this arc
  can make — only "SGD 3e-3 reproduces the historical regime."
- **Median |B@A| is not a reliable standalone predictor.** The lr 1e-4 ladder inverted the
  predicted ordering (least drift → worst completion), and AdamW 1e-3 `step_0020` sat 31×
  above the good SGD checkpoint and still conversed. It is a regime descriptor here, not
  a selection criterion. Rollout behaviour remains ground truth.

# cc-14.0 — Stage-2 SFT: consolidated results (2026-09-01 → 09-08)

Single document for the whole Stage-2 arc. Detail lives in
[cc-12.0](cc-12.0-sgd-reversion.md) (the SGD reversion) and
[cc-13.0](cc-13.0-multidomain-sgd.md) (multi-domain + top-K + noise floors).

---

## 1. The headline

**Light Stage-2 SFT buys ~20 points of task completion. Training to the SFT optimum
costs you ~20 more and lands you well *below* the untrained model. Which thought corpus
you use makes no measurable difference — including using no thoughts at all.**

Insurance, 40 held-out tasks, base (no Stage-2) = **30.0%**:

| checkpoint | completion | vs base |
|---|---|---|
| `step_0020` (light fit) | **47.5 – 55.0%** across every arm | **+18 to +25** |
| `step_best` (PPL-optimal) | **10.0 – 20.0%** across every arm | **-10 to -20** |

Pooled over 3 seeds x `thoughts_policy`: `step_0020` 84/160 = 52.5%, `step_best`
24/160 = 15.0%, **Fisher p = 1.1e-12**.

## 2. Fit depth is the variable — not the optimizer, not the corpus

The retail PPL/behaviour curve (cc-12.0), one domain, one arm family:

| checkpoint | eval action-PPL | completion |
|---|---|---|
| base | 3.8428 | 14.3% |
| SGD 1e-3 `step_0020` | 3.0847 | 31.0% |
| SGD 1e-3 `step_best` | 2.4625 | 23.8% |
| SGD 3e-3 `step_best` | 2.2822 | 9.5% |
| AdamW 1e-3 `step_0020` | 1.7828 | 11.9% |
| AdamW 1e-3 `step_best` | 1.7150 | **0.0%** |

Completion peaks at a *very light* fit and declines monotonically as PPL improves.

**The optimizer is not protective.** SGD 3e-3 at PPL 2.28 scored 9.5% — *worse* than
AdamW at PPL 1.78 (11.9%) despite fitting less far. The earlier "SGD checkpoints were
good" story was under-fitting in disguise; SGD is just a clumsy early stop.

**Confirmed out-of-sample.** Before running it, we predicted a checkpoint reached by a
completely different path (1000 batches @1e-3, PPL 2.2433) would score like SGD 3e-3
(PPL 2.2822, 9.5%) because completion depends on *how far you fit*, not how you got
there. It scored **7.1%** — 2.4 pts apart, inside noise.

**SGD cannot reach AdamW's fit.** 1000 batches took PPL only 2.4625 -> 2.2433 vs AdamW's
1.767, and never triggered early stopping. Both SGD LRs asymptote near ~2.25.

## 3. The corpus does not matter

Insurance `step_0020`, every arm, n=40 each:

| arm | completion |
|---|---|
| `thoughts_policy` (4 seeds) | 52.5 / 55.0 / 47.5 / 55.0 |
| `actions_only` | 50.0, 50.0 |
| `expert_thoughts_all` | 47.5 |
| `thoughts_base` | 47.5 |
| top-K (g8, K=1/2/4/8) | 37.5 / 50.0 / 45.0 / 52.5 |

**Variance decomposition across the 8 arms:** between-arm sd = **4.6 pt**, within-arm
(seed) sd = **3.1 pt** => excess attributable to the arm = **3.4 pt** against a 48% mean.
Choosing a corpus moves the result about as much as re-running one unchanged checkpoint
with a different seed.

Sub-results, each a clean null or near-null:

- **top-K (how many thoughts per action to distil).** Two domains, K spanning 1->8 from a
  `group_size 8` relabel. PPL is monotone but negligible (retail 2.4454->2.4293 over K=1->4;
  insurance 3.0170->3.0095 over K=1->8, a **0.0075** range across an 8x data increase).
  Rollouts indistinguishable: top1 vs top8 **p=0.261**, top1 vs pooled K>=2 **p=0.272**.
  **Best-of-1 is as good as keeping all 8.**
- **`expert_thoughts_all` (human expert reasoning).** *Best fit in 3 of 4 domains* —
  retail 2.2837 vs `thoughts_policy` 2.4625; insurance 2.5750 vs 2.9952 (a 0.42 gap) —
  and rolls out **slightly worse** (47.5 vs 52.5, 10.0 vs 15.0). A large PPL gain bought
  no behavioural gain.
- **`thoughts_base` (E-step scored by the frozen base model).** Fits *worse* than
  policy-scored in all 3 domains (airline +0.130, retail +0.087, insurance +0.003) and
  rolls out the same (47.5 vs 52.5). The EM reward is **not** degrading as the policy
  drifts; the adapting policy is the better judge of which thought makes its own next
  action predictable.
- **Stage-1 corpus change (old `policy` vs `adamw30`).** Null. The old corpus fits
  marginally better (2.4182 vs 2.4625) and rolls out inside the range the new one already
  spans. The asymmetric old-vs-new result that motivated the ablation was noise.

## 4. PPL and agentic competence are decoupled

Every arm that improved the SFT objective failed to improve behaviour, and the
best-fitting checkpoints were the worst behaved:

- `expert_thoughts_all`: -0.42 PPL on insurance, -5 pts completion.
- `step_best` vs `step_0020`: better PPL by construction, **-37.5 pts** completion.
- top-K: monotone PPL gains, flat rollouts.

**`step_best` (PPL-selected) is the wrong checkpoint in every domain measured.** Any
selection criterion based on the Stage-2 objective picks the worse policy.

## 5. Measurement methodology (the part that changed how we read everything)

**Rollout noise floors, measured on *unchanged* checkpoints:**

| harness | n | noise | smallest detectable delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| insurance (snorkel gym) | 40 | 2.5pt same-seed; **sd 3.1** across seeds | ~8 pt |
| retail (tau2) | 42 | **sd 9.0** (31.0/16.7/33.3 — *numerically identical adapters*) | ~18 pt |
| airline (tau2) | 18 | **22.2 pt** (61.1 vs 83.3, same checkpoint) | ~25 pt |
| finance | 10 | floor — 9 of 10 rollouts ever run scored 0/10 | none |

Cause: **tau2 calls an external Claude user simulator we do not seed**; the snorkel gyms
have no simulated user, so they are near-deterministic given a seed (the residual ~2.5pt
is the LLM judge flipping on a borderline answer).

Consequences, now recorded in `CLAUDE.md`:
- **snorkel repeats must vary `--seed`** or they re-derive the same number (40/40 identical).
- **tau2 needs >=3 rollouts per checkpoint**; single-run differences under ~18 pts are
  meaningless.
- Power: detecting a 5pt arm difference needs ~6 runs on insurance, **51 on retail, 77 on
  airline**. Retail/airline can only ever resolve ~15pt effects.

## 6. Claims retracted along the way

Listed because several were reported before the data justified them.

| claim | why it fell |
|---|---|
| "user-turns/episode is the collapse mechanism" | Between-checkpoint it is monotone with completion, but *within* a checkpoint solved and failed episodes are identical (6.20 vs 6.22) or **reversed** (5.22 solved vs 6.39 failed). It separates collapsed from functional policies; it does not predict success. |
| "SGD makes train PPL worse" | Artifact of comparing two single noisy batches. Windowed, every run improves monotonically (2.42 -> 1.79). |
| "the new 3e-3 is worse than the old" | Compared different arms. Like-for-like, `actions_only` went *up* (11.9->16.7) and neither change was significant. |
| "retail `step_0020` > `step_best` is a result" | 31.0 vs 23.8 is inside retail's own 16.6pt same-checkpoint spread. |
| "expert thoughts shift the whole curve up" | Called at 6/10 tasks (60%); the full n=40 came in at 47.5%, *below* `thoughts_policy`. |
| "insurance repeats confirm the effect" | They were same-seed reruns — 40/40 identical. The seed sweep was the real test (and it did confirm it). |

## 7. Bugs found and fixed

| bug | consequence | fix |
|---|---|---|
| fwdproxy began 403-ing huggingface.co | all 4 `expert_thoughts_all` runs + insurance base rollout died in ~25s | `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1` + pinned `HF_HOME` in all 6 launcher scripts |
| `VARIANTS` label mismatch | an 8.5h nb1000 run silently no-op'd in 20s (**a sweep matching zero arms exits 0**) | pass the full label; verify snapshots exist before marking done |
| `task_id` null on snorkel envs | all 40 insurance tasks would collapse into one scoring bucket | handle dict-shaped tasks (`query_id`/`company_task_id`) in `_dump_per_task_records` |
| scorer summed `final_reward` | snorkel `negative_rewards=True` clamps 0 to **-1.0** -> negative count -> `math domain error`, silently swallowed | success = `final_reward > 0`; scorer moved into the repo as `scripts/score_rollouts.py` |
| `rollouts_per_task.jsonl` mixes splits | 44 rows for 42 eval tasks; naive mean gave 29.5-31.7% | filter `split=="eval"`, dedupe on `task_id` |
| trajectories never saved | only scores existed; no way to inspect behaviour | env-gated dump in `_dump_per_task_records` (a cfg key would be silently dropped by `update_configs`) |
| finance ALLDONE gate hardcoded `_v3_` | any run with `CKPT_TAG`/`CKPT_STEP` could never report complete | gate builds the same tag as the rollout loop |
| 6 PID-chained drivers | one early abort cascaded through 3 chains in 10 minutes | single master process running phases in order |
| `CORPUS_VARIANTS=""` | `${VAR:-default}` treats empty as unset -> ran only `k=_last`, matched no arm | bypass the sweep; call `train_sft.sh` directly |

## 8. What is not settled

- **Finance is uninformative.** Not the turn cap (only 2/10 episodes hit it), not a missing
  answer (`respond_user` fires in 8/10), not a broken grader (0 failure warnings) — the 4B
  policy's qualitative `finqa_reasoning` answers are simply judged wrong. Needs the
  quantitative `finqa` split (290 boxed-numeric questions) before finance means anything.
- **Why light Stage-2 helps at all.** We know *that* ~b20 beats base by ~20 pts and that
  the corpus is irrelevant. We do not know what those 640 gradient steps actually fix
  (format adherence? tool-call syntax? something about the hide-observations regime?).
  An `actions_only` vs base behavioural diff at b20 would be the cheapest probe.
- **Where the peak is.** `step_0020` is the earliest snapshot we roll out; `save_every 10`
  keeps `step_0010`, which has never been evaluated. The optimum may be earlier than b20.
- **base-vs-policy at proper power.** 4 extra insurance seeds on `thoughts_base` are
  queued (n=5 vs n=4), enough to test the observed 5pt gap.

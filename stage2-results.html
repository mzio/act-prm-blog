Stage-2 SFT: when you stop matters, what you distil doesn't

Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 · LoRA r8/a16 · SGD 1e-3 · sft_flat, 200 batches × 32 steps · hide-observations · four domains (tau2 retail/airline, Snorkel finance/insurance). Runs 2026-09-01 → 09-08. Full detail in notes/cc-12.0, cc-13.0, cc-14.0.

The result. A lightly fine-tuned Stage-2 checkpoint beats the untrained model by roughly 20 points of task completion. Continuing to the perplexity optimum — the checkpoint any standard early-stopping rule would select — costs about 20 more and lands below the untrained model. Across eight different thought corpora, which thoughts you distil changes almost nothing.

1. Every corpus behaves the same; only the stopping point matters

Insurance is the domain used for every quantitative claim here, because it is the only one with the measurement precision to support one (§4). Base model, no Stage-2: 30.0% (12/40).

Pooled over three seeds of thoughts_policy: step_0020 84/160 = 52.5% vs step_best 24/160 = 15.0%, Fisher p = 1.1×10−12.

Variance decomposition across the eight arms: between-arm sd = 4.6 pt, within-arm (seed) sd = 3.1 pt, so the excess attributable to the choice of corpus is 3.4 pt against a 48% mean. Picking a corpus moves the result about as much as re-running one unchanged checkpoint under a different seed.

2. The SFT objective is anti-correlated with agentic competence

Two independent knobs — learning rate and optimiser — trace the same curve, which is what makes it a statement about fit depth rather than about either knob:

  • The optimiser is not protective. SGD at 3e-3 (PPL 2.28) scored 9.5%, worse than AdamW at PPL 1.78 (11.9%) despite fitting less far. Earlier results where SGD "looked good" were under-fitting in disguise.
  • Confirmed out-of-sample. We predicted, before running it, that a checkpoint reached by a completely different route (1000 batches @1e-3, PPL 2.2433) would score like SGD 3e-3 (PPL 2.2822, 9.5%). It scored 7.1%.
  • Perplexity gains do not transfer. expert_thoughts_all fits insurance 0.42 PPL better than thoughts_policy and rolls out slightly worse.
Practical consequence. step_best — selected on eval perplexity — is the wrong checkpoint in every domain we measured. Any selection rule based on the Stage-2 objective picks the worse policy.

3. Training curves

How many thoughts per action should you distil?

From a group_size 8 relabel we exported the best 1, 2, 4 and 8 candidates per action — identical task coverage, only the number of distilled thoughts changing. Perplexity improves monotonically with K and the total effect is negligible: retail 2.4454 → 2.4293 (K=1→4), insurance 3.0170 → 3.0095 (K=1→8), a 0.0075 range across an 8× data increase. Rollouts are indistinguishable (top-1 vs top-8, p = 0.261). Best-of-1 is as good as keeping all eight.

4. Why only one domain gets to make claims

τ²-bench drives its user turns with an external LLM simulator that we do not seed, so two runs of the same weights genuinely differ. The Snorkel gyms have no simulated user — only tools and a judge — and are near-deterministic given a seed (the residual ~2.5 pt is the judge flipping on a borderline answer).

6. What we still don't know

  • Why light Stage-2 helps at all. We know ~640 gradient steps buy ~20 points and that the corpus is irrelevant — but not what those steps fix. Format adherence? Tool-call syntax? An actions_only-vs-base behavioural diff at b20 is the cheapest probe.
  • Where the peak is. step_0020 is the earliest checkpoint we roll out; step_0010 exists and has never been evaluated.
  • Finance is uninformative — not the turn cap, not a missing answer, not a broken grader; the 4B policy's qualitative answers are simply judged wrong. Needs the quantitative finqa split.
